Case Selected: 
Amazon developed an internal AI-powered recruiting tool to automate resume screening. However, the system learned gender bias from historical hiring data and systematically penalized women candidates, leading to its eventual shutdown. 
Context :Internal hiring system at Amazon (2014–2017). Resume screening for technical roles (e.g., software engineers). Ranking candidates using a 1–5 star scoring system 
Failure Description :  Bias and penalized the resume which contain women related applications.machine-learning specialists uncovered a big problem: their new recruiting engine did not like women.
Specifics over slogans:  The system did things wrong as it Penalized resumes containing: Words like “women’s” (e.g., women’s chess club) . Favored resumes resembling male-dominated historical profiles. Failed to evaluate candidates gender-neutrally. Reinforced existing hiring bias instead of removing it

Mechanism and root cause: Model trained on 10 years of historical resumes. Majority of resumes came from male candidates ,No fairness constraints or bias correction mechanisms.  Optimization focused only on pattern matching, not fairness

Impact and ethics:   Explainability tools, Audit pipelines during development,Data reflects male dominance in tech hiring.Penalizes resumes with female indicators.Amazon abandons the system entirely.
Real impact was on Women candidates as they were Unfairly downgraded or filtered out,  Loss of Equal opportunity, lack of Trust in hiring systems.
Reputational risk: Highlighted globally as AI bias example, Slowed adoption of AI in hiring
Project scrapped after years of investment.Ethical Impact :  Trust and Transparency was missing. AI systems perceived as objective, but were biased. Violated equal employment principles


Preventive actions:
Removing gender-indicative terms.Reweighting features,Amazon later introduced tools like bias detection in ML pipelines (e.g., SageMaker Clarify).  Amazon later introduced tools like bias detection in ML pipelines (e.g., SageMaker Clarify)
Data audits before training, Model fairness validation, Responsible AI frameworks and Ethical review boards
Continuous monitoring for Drift , and then LLM-as-judge / fairness evaluators (modern approach).
